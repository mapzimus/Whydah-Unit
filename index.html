The Whydah Gally

Built in London to carry enslaved people. Taken by Sam Bellamy off the Bahamas. Lost in fifteen minutes on a lee shore — and found again with a chart drawn the month she sank.

The voyage, the wreck, the chart

1715 – 1717 – 1984

One ship, three lives.

Before she was a pirate legend she was a slaver, named for the West African port of Ouidah. The gold in her hold was the proceeds of that voyage. The chart that found her was a salvage map. None of that is a footnote.

01 · Slaver

London to Ouidah to Jamaica

Sir Humphrey Morice, MP and London’s foremost slave merchant, commissioned her. 367 people were loaded in West Africa. 312 were sold in Kingston. Fifty-five died on the crossing.

02 · Flagship

Bellamy takes her in three days

Late February 1717, in the Windward Passage. A 300-mile chase. Captain Prince surrendered; Bellamy let him sail away. For ten weeks she was the richest pirate ship of the Golden Age.

03 · Wreck

A nor’easter off Wellfleet

April 26, 1717. 144 of 146 drowned. Cyprian Southack marked the spot that May. Barry Clifford read the same chart in 1984. The wreck is still under that beach.
