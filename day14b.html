The Trial Nobody Told You About

8 minutes · whole crew · then you vote with your feet

The Whydah went down April 26, 1717. Nine men crawled out of that surf alive. Here is what Boston did with them.

Oct 18, 1717. Court convenes in Boston. Eight men on trial for piracy.
Oct 22. Six found guilty. Two walked: Thomas Davis and Thomas South. Both proved they were forced men.
Nov 15. The six are hanged at the Charlestown Ferry gallows, down on the mudflats, below the high tide line.
And the ninth man? John Julian, the Miskito pilot, never got a trial at all. He was sold.

Why the mudflats? Below the tide line was the sea's ground, not the town's. Geography decided who was allowed to hang you. You have been reading that idea all month.

⚑ STAND AND VOTE. Davis said he was forced. So did the six who hanged. Same claim, different endings. Walk to your side of the room:
JUSTICE (the court got it right) or PAPERWORK (they got the ending the record gave them).
Three people say why. Then the big one, out loud: who wrote the story we just read, and who never got to speak in it?
LOG IT: one line at the top of THE TEST page: the fact from this trial you are stealing for your project.

Every name and date above is on the site, on the Timeline and the People tab. You will need them in nine minutes.

Evidence RaidSCORED

9 minutes on the clock · solo or with your partner · Chromebooks up

A beautiful project with nothing behind it is a poster. Raid the site. Come back with three hard facts about YOUR person or YOUR topic, and write where each one came from.

⚑ SCORING, on the board:
  • 1 point per fact with the page it came from. No page, no point.
  • 2 points if it is tagged Solid on the site, or it comes off a real artifact.
  • 3 points for a fact nobody else in the room brings back.
  • Minus 1 for anything you cannot show me on a page. Made up is worse than missing.
Hunting grounds: the Timeline, the People tab, Artifacts, the Wreck pages.
LOG IT: THE RAID page. Three facts. Three sources. Numbers and dates beat adjectives.

Fastest crew to three sourced facts calls the first station pick.

Build a Piece of It

~25 minutes · solo or with your partner · pick YOUR station

Not the whole project. One small real piece of it. Enough to know what the full build will take. Put at least one raided fact into the piece you build.

🗺️ Story map or narrated tour: open Map Studio. Drop your first 3 pins. Write one pin's story in your person's voice. Try the 1717 Chart mode and the globe. Save it. It stays on that machine, so note your Chromebook number.
🖼️ Poster, exhibit board, newspaper: grab scrap paper. Sketch the whole layout small. Every box, every image, every label. This sketch is what writes your supply list.
📖 Flip book, graphic novel, letters: make page one for real. Fold it, draw the first panel, write the first letter. Time it. Then multiply by eight and look at that number.
🎭 Performance or shanty: write your first 6 lines. Say them to a wall. Then to a partner. Time it. Multiply to 3 minutes and find what is missing.
🎬 Slides story: storyboard all 10 to 12 slides on paper. Title, image idea, one quote each. Then see Max about the no login build plan.
LOG IT: THE TEST page. What you built. One thing that worked. One thing that fought you.

Format fought you the whole time? Good. That is the trial run doing its job. Switch to your other format NOW and test that one. Today is the last day switching is free.

Shipyard Inspection

7 minutes · everybody on their feet · silent

Tools down. Leave your build on your desk with your pitch line face up. Now walk the shipyard. No talking. You are an inspector.

  1. Visit at least three builds that are not near you.
  2. Leave two slips on two different builds. One says STEAL THIS: the thing you want to copy. One says IT NEEDS: the thing they forgot.
  3. Back at your desk: read your slips. Add anything true to THE LIST.

Best slip in the room gets read out loud at the ring. Sign your slips.

4

THE LIST, and you are on a budget

7 minutes · solo · this is the one Max actually needs

You built a piece of it, so now you know what the real thing needs. Write it exact, boring, complete. Then price it.

  1. Every item, with a number. Board size. How many markers. What colors. Which pictures, named. Glue. String. Tape. "Art stuff" buys nothing. "One poster board, four black markers, one glue stick" builds a project.
  2. Guess the price of each one. Then add it up.
  3. Your budget is $15. Partners get $25. Over budget? Cut something, or write one sentence telling Max why it is worth it. He reads every one.
  4. All digital? Write ALL DIGITAL big, then list what you still need from Max. A printed map. A practice slot. A machine held for you.
LOG IT: THE LIST page is tomorrow's form, already written. You copy it over in ink and you are done in two minutes.

Pitch Drill30 SECONDS

6 minutes · standing · you say it out loud today so tomorrow is not the first time
  1. Find a partner who is NOT your project partner. 30 seconds each: your pitch line, your person, one raided fact. Timed.
  2. New partner. Do it again, faster and better. It gets better every single time, which is the point.
  3. Three volunteers pitch to the whole room. The room times you and calls it: did that land?

Nail it in under 30 seconds tomorrow and the Captain's Check is a formality.
