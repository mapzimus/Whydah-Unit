## Project Overview

| **Adopt a Ship** <br>→ https://whydahstory.com/day4.html <br><br>**Activities:** Absorbed the missed Day 4 plus a tightened Atlantic-World lesson. ### Week 3 (07/20) — Life as a Pirate

**Focus / KSQ 3:** How does geography shape the choices people can make?

| Mon 7/20 · **Day 8** | Tue 7/21 · **Day 9** | Wed 7/22 · **Days 10 + 12** | Thu 7/23 · **Day 11 — FIELD TRIP 3** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **The Vote** <br>→ https://whydahstory.com/day8.html <br><br>**Activities:** A live class vote first, bodies across the room, then the debrief: in summer 1716 Bellamy's crew voted Benjamin Hornigold out for refusing to attack English ships, and elected Bellamy. A farmhand could not fire a lord; a Navy sailor could not fire an admiral; a pirate crew could fire its captain. Students then **pick one of eight real crew members** — Bellamy, Paulsgrave Williams, John Julian, John King, and the hanged six — and build a **push/pull T-chart** from the evidence, minimum three on each side. Students read the closest surviving analogue, **Bartholomew Roberts' eleven articles (c. 1721)**, and answer three questions with rule numbers: which rule surprises you, which protects the weakest person aboard, which one would you strike. Rule Hunt for points. Then crews of 3–4 **write their own Articles**, the whole class votes them into force, every student signs the master copy, and it is sealed with **real red wax** and hung on the wall until the Showcase. Muster circle to close. <br><br>**Reflection Q:** Would you sign on to a set of articles like these? What would worry you? <br><br>**Academic Vocab:** articles · covenant · vote · shares | **The Wreck** → https://whydahstory.com/day10.html <br>**Lost and Found** → https://whydahstory.com/day12.html <br><br>**Activities (Day 10):** *Draw the storm before you see any evidence* — 12:15 AM, April 26, 1717, a nor'easter driving the ship onto a lee shore, 146 people aboard — then test the drawing against the record. Bellamy was sailing four ships that night with four different endings. She struck a bar ~500 ft off the beach; 2 of 146 reached the sand alive: Thomas Davis and John Julian, 16. Written **land acknowledgment** for Punonakanit Wampanoag territory, whose voice is absent from the 1717 record. Wreck mapped at **41.891°N, 69.957°W**. <br><br>**Activities (Day 12):** *One Map, 267 Years* — Southack's salvage mission failed, but the mark he made on his chart found the ship in 1984. Students then run a **6×6 grid archaeology dig** (A1–F6), logging every find by its square, and learn provenance: *where* a thing was found is what makes it evidence instead of loot. <br><br>**Reflection Qs:** If the Whydah had not wrecked, would we know any of these people's names? · Why does evidence matter for the story you tell? <br><br>**Academic Vocab:** wreck · survivor · evidence · land acknowledgment · salvage · grid · archaeology | **Georges Island / Boston Harbor** <br>→ https://whydahstory.com/day11.html <br><br>**Activities:** Ferry out into Boston Harbor — the gateway every ship had to pass, and the city where the story ended for the men who lived. The captured crew were tried in Boston; six were hanged at the Charlestown Ferry in November 1717. On the water: spot **Nix's Mate**, where the pirate William Fly was gibbeted in 1726 — nine years later, a different chapter, and the pages say so plainly, because keeping the two true stories separate *is* the history skill. Fort Warren read as a later layer (begun 1833). Harbor sketch-map on site. The Captain's Log breaks its own rule today: written **in a chosen historical voice**, not the adopted ship's captain — the first live rehearsal of the final project. <br><br>**Reflection Q:** Standing at the harbor gateway, how does where a story ends change the way you'd tell it? <br><br>**Academic Vocab:** gateway · channel · gibbet · legend |

**Weekly extended writing prompt:** Pick one person who died in the wreck or stood trial. Write a paragraph about who you imagine they were.

### Week 4 (07/27) — Modern Oceans, and the Record

**Focus / KSQ 3 → KSQ 4:** Whose stories get mapped, and whose get buried — across time?

| Mon 7/27 · **Day 13** | Tue 7/28 · **Day 14** | Wed 7/29 · **Day 15** | Thu 7/30 · **Day 16** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Where Ships Squeeze Through** <br>→ https://whydahstory.com/day13.html <br><br>**Activities:** Zoom out and the ocean looks free; it is not. Using our own **Chokepoints map**, students hunt four doors — **Suez** (~12% of world trade), **Malacca** (about a third of global shipping), **Bab-el-Mandeb / Red Sea** (attacks since 2023 pushing crews the long way around Africa), and **Panama** (the 2023–24 drought that cut daily transits by about a third). For each: what makes the squeeze — land on both sides, a canal, a strait? Then students **hand-rule a navigator's plotting sheet** — grid and compass rose — exactly as a 1717 navigator ruled up a fresh page. Frame: piracy clusters where three factors line up — a waterway, unpoliced water, and coastal economic deprivation. 1717 and today are the same machine, not a coincidence. <br><br>**Reflection Q:** What does where someone chooses to sail tell you about what they're running from — or toward? <br><br>**Academic Vocab:** chokepoint · sea lane · dead reckoning · hotspot | **Salem Then and Now** <br>→ https://whydahstory.com/day14.html <br><br>**Activities:** Derby Wharf (built 1762, nearly half a mile) beside the same water today on the live traffic map — what would a sailor from the age of sail still recognize, and what would stop them cold? Then the harder page of the local ledger, taught factually and without hand-wringing: Salem got rich on the water, cod and lumber out to Caribbean plantations worked by enslaved people, sugar and molasses and rum back — and after the Revolution some Salem merchants sent slave ships of their own. Named ships, named owners, surviving record. **Trace one item:** each student picks something from their own backpack, finds where it was made, sketches a rough world map from memory, and plots the route and probable ship type. <br><br>**Reflection Q:** What's still happening today that started in 1717? <br><br>**Academic Vocab:** wharf · container · import · global trade | **The Trial** <br>→ https://whydahstory.com/day14b.html <br><br>**Activities:** The room becomes the Boston Admiralty court of October 1717 and the students are the commissioners. The record is real: *The Trials of Eight Persons*, printed Boston, 1718. **Scored evidence raid (9 min):** bring back three pieces of evidence *with the page each came from* — 1 point per sourced fact, 2 if it comes from a sworn deposition or a real artifact, 3 for evidence nobody else brings back, **minus 1 for anything you cannot show on a page.** In this court, made up is perjury. **Verdict round:** four real men — Thomas Davis, Thomas South, Peter Cornelius Hoof, and Simon van der Vorst — each heard and voted on: GUILTY or FORCED MAN, vote with your feet, defend if called, then Max reads what the court actually did in 1717. Almost every man at the bar said *I was forced*; the court believed two. The day closes on **the ninth man**: John Julian, the Miskito pilot, got no verdict to vote on — no trial, no recorded defense. He was sold. *Eight men got a trial. One man got a receipt.* That is the hinge into the final project, which is framed from here on as **the appeal**. <br><br>**Reflection Q:** The court of 1717 gave my person ___. My appeal gives them ___. <br><br>**Academic Vocab:** deposition · testimony · verdict · forced man · perjury | **Pitch Day** <br>→ https://whydahstory.com/day15.html <br><br>**Activities:** Yesterday the record decided how a person is remembered; today the student takes the record's job, and the project becomes *the appeal*. Last pass through the People and format menus. Write **the pitch** — WHO · HOW · WHAT FROM · WHY ME — then a 4–6 sentence paragraph naming all four, with the rule that you must be able to physically touch the Journal page behind every source you claim. **Sharpening round:** pitch aloud for one minute, partner asks one question that finds the soft spot, revise. Day ends with a **signed commitment form** in Max's hand: person, format, evidence, supply list. Students rule the form up by hand — there is no classroom printer. That sheet is the contract for the build days and the shopping list; supplies are bought from it once. <br><br>**Reflection Q:** Why did you choose the perspective you chose? <br><br>**Academic Vocab:** pitch · perspective · format · source |

**Weekly extended writing prompt:** What is one reason to leave and one reason to join that you think mattered most for the Whydah's crew? Support with evidence.

| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Synthesis Studio** <br>→ https://whydahstory.com/day16.html <br><br>**Activities:** One last voyage through the whole story, sailed as the chosen person. Students walk in *as* their person (ten seconds, eyes closed — what would they notice about this room, and what would they beg us to get right?). Then **The Hunt: Four That Matter** — choose four artifacts or moments from the Artifacts tab, the Timeline, the People tab, and **their own five-week folder**, which today gets renamed *your archive*. The tracker becomes tomorrow's build plan. <br><br>**Reflection Q:** Looking back as your chosen person — what did you notice this time that you missed the first time? <br><br>**Academic Vocab:** synthesis · perspective · archive · buried story | **Build Day 1** <br>→ https://whydahstory.com/day17.html <br><br>**Activities:** Launch circle — one sentence each, a promise to the crew: "Today I build ___." Then straight to work, with a written milestone at the top of the build sheet. *Finish ugly today; polish tomorrow. Start with the hardest piece, not the prettiest.* Mid-session mini-workshop, **"Give them a voice"**: every invented line must stand on a documented fact — write the fact, note its source, then write the line. Worked example: the record says John King, aged eight to eleven, demanded to join the pirates off the *Bonetta* on 9 November 1716 and his mother could not stop him. "My mother held my arm. I chose the pirates anyway" stands on that. "I always dreamed of being famous" floats — cut it or find a fact it can stand on. Drop-in help stations: tech, writing, performance. Milestone board. <br><br>**Reflection Q:** What's the hardest part of telling this story? <br><br>**Academic Vocab:** draft · evidence · voice | **Build Day 2 — Peer Review & Polish** <br>→ https://whydahstory.com/day18.html <br><br>**Activities:** Structured peer review in deliberately mismatched pairs — your partner is not your best friend, and builds in a different format, because fresh eyes catch what yours skip. Six steps: SHOW (two minutes, then quiet) · "I noticed…" (one exact thing that works) · "I wonder…" (one question a visitor might ask) · "What if…" (one change) · WRITE + THANKS (the maker records the partner's words *verbatim* and signs it) · SWAP. Reviewer's code: one question, one suggestion, not five — choosing is the skill. Makers do not defend; they write it down and rule on it in the next block. Then **fix their thing first**, revise, rehearse, and set up the Showcase space together. <br><br>**Reflection Q:** What did your partner see in your work that you couldn't see yourself? <br><br>**Academic Vocab:** peer review · revision · protocol · run of show | **THE SHOWCASE** <br>→ https://whydahstory.com/day19.html <br><br>**Activities:** No missions, no races, no points. *You are not a student today. You are the museum.* **Set-up (15 min)** — projects to stations facing the room, name cards (your name, your person, your format), Voyage Journals open on the display table. **Showcase (50 min)** — doors open to families, LEAP staff, museum guests, and the public. Guests get a story, not a speech: your person and why you chose them, what you made and how, and one surprise. Then questions. *"I don't know" is a real answer — historians say it all the time. Say what you know, say what nobody knows, and say the difference.* **Closing circle (25 min)** — Journals in hand, one last log entry in the captain's voice: where does your ship sail from here? <br><br>**Reflection Q:** What did you choose to include in your project — and what did you choose to leave out? <br><br>**Academic Vocab:** showcase · audience · reflection |

**Additional notes:** No grades — there never were. The Showcase *is* the product. A/V is needed for the performance and narrated-tour formats; room layout, family invites, and the closing-circle script are on the prep checklist.

## Assessment

No grades, by program design. Evidence of thinking, in three layers:

1. **The Voyage Journal** — a folder with one deliverable per session (maps, analysis cards, T-charts, the storm drawing, the signed Articles with its wax seal, the dig log, the pitch). By Week 5 it holds 16+ dated entries and is renamed *the archive*. It is both the running record and the evidence bank for the final project.
2. **The final project** — one person, one format, citing **3–4 named Journal entries**. A single one-page rubric works across all twelve formats → https://whydahstory.com/handouts.html
3. **The public Showcase** — presentation to a real outside audience, with Journals displayed gallery-walk style.
