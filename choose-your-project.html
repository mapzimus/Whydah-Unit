How this works

Read this once. It's the whole deal.
  1. Your person — someone real from our story. Their cards live in the People tab.
  2. Your format — ONE from the menu below. It's how your person's story gets told at the Showcase on Thursday, August 6.
  3. Your evidence — 3–4 pages from your own Voyage Journal. No journal pages, no project.
  4. Thursday, July 30: you pitch it, Max checks it, and you hand in the commitment form at the bottom of this page. If your project needs supplies, that form is how they get bought. No form, no supplies.

Only two build days (Aug 4 + 5). Pick something you can actually finish. Small and done beats big and half-built — every time.

The menu: twelve ways to tell it

💻 = on the Chromebooks (no logins needed) · ✂️ = physical, Max buys the supplies · 🎭 = performed live

🗺️ Story map 💻 or ✂️

8–10 stops along your person's voyage, each with a pin and their voice. Build it in our own Map Studio — pick your person, drop starter pins, write in their voice, cite Journal pages, then print a story sheet or run a live tour.

Needs: Map Studio (free, on this site) or a big sheet + markers.

🖼️ Illustrated poster ✂️

A visual timeline or map with your written reflection built in. The classic, for a reason.

Needs: poster board, markers, maybe printed images.

🎭 Live performance 🎭

3–5 minute monologue or two-person scene, performed at the Showcase, in your person's voice.

Needs: a script and guts. Optional: simple costume bits.

📖 Character flip book ✂️

Handmade 8-page folded book written in your character's voice. Starter pages are in your folder.

Needs: paper, staples, colored pencils.

✉️ Letter sequence ✂️

4–6 letters in character, tracking one perspective across the voyage. Seal them like it's 1717.

Needs: paper, pen; optional wax seal (we have it).

🏛️ Museum exhibit board ✂️

Artifacts (made or printed) with real museum labels, curating your person's story like the Real Pirates museum does.

Needs: board, label cards, artifact materials.

💥 Graphic novel spread ✂️

4–6 illustrated panels telling ONE scene from your person's life. Dialogue counts as evidence if it's sourced.

Needs: paper or board, pencils, fine-liners.

📰 1717 newspaper front page ✂️

A broadside reporting the wreck or a capture, period style — headlines, columns, an "eyewitness" quote.

Needs: big paper; we can tea-stain it.

🎬 Slides story 💻

10–12 chapter slides with images, quotes, and evidence — built offline-style on our machines, presented from Max's account at the Showcase.

Needs: a Chromebook and a plan. Talk to Max first — our machines have no logins.

🌍 Narrated map tour 💻+🎭

You drive the Map Studio 🎤 Tour mode (or the 3D flythrough) live at the Showcase while narrating in character. A performance with a map.

Needs: your route built in Map Studio + practiced narration with Tour.

🎵 Ballad or sea shanty 🎭

Original lyrics in period style telling your person's story. Perform it, or print it beautifully with a verse-by-verse sources list.

Needs: paper. Courage optional but encouraged.

⭐ Pitch your own ?

Needs: a straight answer to "can you finish it in two days?"

The cast: whose story will you tell?

Every project needs a PERSON. Twelve good doors into the same shipwreck — full cards live in the People tab.

🏴‍☠️ Sam Bellamy

The captain everyone knows — which is exactly the trap. Can you show the parts of him the legend leaves out?

👦 John King

Boy pirate, age 8–11. CHOSE the pirate life over his mother's ship. His shoe and leg bone came off the wreck. You've seen them.

🧭 John Julian

Ship's pilot, likely Miskito. Steered the Whydah, survived the wreck at ~16 — then was sold into slavery. The record follows him for years.

🪓 Thomas Davis

Welsh carpenter, forced aboard. Survived the wreck, stood trial for piracy in Boston — and had to prove he never chose it.

⛵ Paulsgrave Williams

Bellamy's partner-captain. Middle-aged, wealthy family, chose piracy anyway — and sailed AWAY from the storm that killed his friend. Then vanished.

🌊 "Maria Hallett"

The Cape Cod legend: Bellamy's love, watching the wreck from the dunes. CONTESTED — the record barely knows her. Perfect for a legend-vs-evidence project.

🏝️ Philip Ashton

Marblehead fisherman, seized by pirates, refused to join, escaped, and survived TWO YEARS alone on an island. Came home to a town that thought him dead.

🗺️ Cyprian Southack

The mapmaker who arrived too late to salvage anything — except the wreck's location, in ink. His mark found the Whydah 267 years later.

🤿 Barry Clifford

The Cape Cod diver who believed the legend, read Southack's chart, and in 1984 brought up the bell with the ship's name on it.

💰 Sir Humphrey Morice

The London MP whose money built the Whydah as a slave ship. Never touched a deck. The machine's quietest, most powerful man.

⚖️ John Baker or Robert Auchmuty

A pirate hanged in Boston — or the defense lawyer who resigned in protest at his trial. The courtroom end of the story.

❓ Someone never named

Dozens aboard — including African crew members freed or taken from prizes — left no name in the record. Telling an unnamed person's story honestly is the hardest, bravest pick on this list.

THE PERSON TEST: Do they pull at you? · Are there Journal pages and People-tab evidence to build from? · Can you say one true, specific thing about them right now? Three yeses = your person.
