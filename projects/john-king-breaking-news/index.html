Breaking News: John King and the Savage report

A newspaper front page on John King built from the primary source itself — Abijah Savage, the Bonetta's commander, reporting that the boy demanded to join the pirates. Hand-lettered and bordered in marker, signed along the edge by three students (names to follow once we confirm the spellings).

The front page

Handwritten text is transcribed with the students' wording, spelling, and punctuation preserved; letter case is normalized for legibility. Scribbled-out words the students edited away are noted in brackets, as they left them visible on the page.

Masthead (hand-lettered, orange and brown)

Breaking News

The report

John King, then aged between eight and eleven, was a passenger on the Bonetta. According to Abijah Savage, the Bonetta's commander, the pirate looted the ship for 15 days during which time King demanded to join Bellamy's crew [scribbled-out word] from being forced [scribbled-out word] or compelled to join. Savage wrote a report: "He declaird he would kill him self if he was a restraid or [scribbled-out word] threatend his mother."

Decoration

The masthead is drawn in two-tone bubble letters over double rules, the whole page bordered in yellow and brown marker, with red X marks in the corners. The word "time" carries an inserted letter above a caret — an edit made mid-sentence and left visible, like the scribbles.

This one goes straight to the primary source. Abijah Savage, commander of the sloop Bonetta, really did give sworn testimony after Bellamy released his ship in November 1716, and the students' quote tracks his deposition closely: King was "so far from being forced or compelled" that he "declared he would kill himself if he was restrained, and even threatened his mother." Even their editing scribbles echo how depositions get quoted and requoted. The full account is on King's card at People, and how the class weighed testimony like Savage's is at Methods. It is part of the class's ever-growing John King shelf — see the whole collection from the projects page.
