John King: The Boy Pirate

By Erick. A poster on John King, the youngest member of the Whydah's crew: two researched information panels, a colored-pencil portrait, and a hand-drawn map of the Bonetta's interrupted voyage.

The poster, panel by panel

Handwritten text is transcribed with the student's wording, spelling, and punctuation preserved; letter case is normalized for legibility.

Title (marker, across the top)

John King: The Boy Pirate

Information panel 1 (pencil, top left)

John King: The Boy Pirate-Information:

  • Passenger on merchant sloop Bonetta with his mother (Jamaica-Antigua)
  • Bellamy captured the Bonetta Nov 9, 1716: looted her for 15 days
  • King demanded to join the pirates, threatened to kill himself if restrained, threatened his mother

Portrait (colored pencil, top right)

John King as Erick drew him: a boy in a black tricorn hat with a skull-and-crossbones emblem, a red-and-blue striped shirt with a knotted red neckerchief, blue trousers — and yellow-and-black sneakers.

Information panel 2 (pencil, bottom left)

John King-information

  • Died in the Whydah wrek, age at most 11
  • Remains identified 2006: 11-in fibula in a shoe and silk stocking

Map (pencil and colored pencil, bottom right)

A hand-drawn route map of the voyage the pirates interrupted: Jamaica and Antigua in green, Hispaniola sketched faintly in pencil (labeled Haiti / Dominican Republic), and a route line traced through the Caribbean Sea. The labels, at their dots:

  • Jamaica — "Where Bonetta came from"
  • Caribbean Sea — "Where Bellamy captured the Bonetta Nov 9, 1716; looted her for 15 days"
  • Antigua — "Where the Bonetta was supposed to go"
The history on this poster is solid. The information panels follow the documented record of John King — the Bonetta, November 9, 1716, the demand to join, and the 2006 identification of his remains by an 11-inch fibula still in its shoe and silk stocking. The map plots the capture as geography: where the sloop sailed from, where she was bound, and where Bellamy stopped her. And the real King is known to us today by his shoe — Erick drew him in sneakers.
Presented at the Navigating Piracy Showcase, August 6, 2026, at Collins Middle School, Salem.
