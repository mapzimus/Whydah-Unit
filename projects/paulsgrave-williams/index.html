So no one may forget: Paulsgrave Williams

A writing piece on Paulsgrave Williams — the wealthy silversmith who didn't need piracy and chose it anyway, missed the wreck by visiting family, and walked out of history. The title is the student's own opening line. No name is written on the work.

The piece

Transcribed with the student's wording, spelling, and punctuation preserved; letter case is normalized for legibility.

I am writing this so no one may forget the story of Paullgrave Williams. The wealthy silver smith didn't even need to become a pirate, he did it for the thrill. Born in 1675, he joined Black Sam's crew in 1716. Just one year later, he became the capitain of the Marianne, Sam's companion ship. Before the Whydah made it to Cape Cod, Paul asked if he could visit some family while the rest of the crew sailed on, assuming they would sail back to pick him up. He waited two weeks for a man he didn't know was already dead. Eventually he sailed back to Nassau to continue raiding. In 1718 he accepts the royal pardon before deciding piracy is really fun and goes back in 1720. And then he disappears. We will probably never know how his story ends.

Every beat of this is documented — and the best sentence is true. Williams really was a wealthy Rhode Island silversmith born around 1675 who chose piracy anyway; he really captained the Marianne; and he really was away seeing family when the storm took the Whydah, then waited about two weeks at the rendezvous at Damariscove Island, Maine — "for a man he didn't know was already dead," as the student puts it, which is as good a line as any historian has written about it. The pardon in 1718, the return to raiding, and the vanishing are all in the record too: Williams is the pirate who got away clean, and his open ending is exactly why the class's People page recommends his perspective. The wreck he missed is at The Wreck.
Presented at the Navigating Piracy Showcase, August 6, 2026, at Collins Middle School, Salem.
