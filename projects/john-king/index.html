“Hello my name is John King”

By Erick. A first-person account of John King, the youngest member of the Whydah's crew. Handwritten and mounted with a print of Gregory Manchess's painting of King. Erick also made a poster on King.

Transcription

Transcribed from the original. The student's wording, spelling, and punctuation are preserved; letter case is normalized for legibility.

Hello my name is John King I'm 8 or 11 years old I was born in the year 1705 or 1708 and I died on April 26 1717

John King was born in a small coastal village his father was a sailor and from a young age John dreamed of, the sea he would often listen to his father's tales of adventure dreaming of setting sail one day. one fateful day, John's village was attacked by the infamous pirate Bback Sam Bellamy the pirates took everything and John's father was captured filled with fear but also excitement. John decided to join the pirates. life on the pirate ship was tough but thrilling. John quickly adapted learning the ropes and gaining the respect of the crew. despite his young age he showed remarkable. courage and wit. which caught the eye of Black Sam Bellamy himself John's bravery and quick thinking during raids. earned him a special place among the pirates. Black Sam Bellamy saw great potential in him. and began to teach him the art of piracy. John learned how to navigate

The text ends here, mid-sentence, at the bottom edge of the sheet.

The documented John King was a passenger on the sloop Bonetta with his mother when Bellamy captured her on November 9, 1716 — and demanded to join the pirates. He died in the wreck at age 8–11; his remains were identified in 2006 by an 11-inch fibula still in its shoe and silk stocking. The village, the captured father, and the apprenticeship are this writer's invention — the unit asked students to build a voice on the documented facts, and to own the gaps.
