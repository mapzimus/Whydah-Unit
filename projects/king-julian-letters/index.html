Letters between John King and John Julian

An imagined correspondence between the Whydah's two youngest crew members — John King, the boy pirate, writes first, and John Julian, the teenage pilot, writes back. Both letters are handwritten and folded like real mail, signed in character. No student name is written on the work.

The letters

Transcribed with the students' wording, spelling, and punctuation preserved; letter case is normalized for legibility.

From John King

Dear Julian,

Hello, I heard you were young when you survived slavery. I'm a pirate who is also young. I am willing to give you gold, if you'd like. I would love to hear more about you.

Sincerely, John King

From John Julian

Dear John King,

Hello it's nice to hear from you. But your gold is not my property. I want to thank you for your offer but no thank you. You are younger than me. I heard that you wanted to know how I'm doing. I would love to hear more about you too. I'm doing good.

—Julian

The two children of the Whydah, writing to each other. They were real shipmates: King, somewhere between eight and eleven, who demanded to join the pirates; and Julian, the sixteen-year-old Miskito pilot who steered Bellamy's flagship. The letters get their ages right — Julian was older, as his reply notes — and they get something harder right too. The student has Julian refuse King's gold: "your gold is not my property." In real history the refusal ran the other way — Julian survived the wreck only to be treated as property himself, sold into slavery in Boston. Whether the echo is deliberate or instinct, it is the unit's deepest question in two sentences. Their true stories are side by side at People, and what became of the survivors at Salem.
Presented at the Navigating Piracy Showcase, August 6, 2026, at Collins Middle School, Salem.
