John King, the youngest known pirate

By Kelly and Jasmely — their second piece in the archive, alongside The Story of Albus Sharktooth. Two sheets on John King: a pencil drawing of the boy pirate with a researched profile, and a page of bulleted notes.

The fact sheet, sheet by sheet

Handwritten text is transcribed with the students' wording, spelling, and punctuation preserved; letter case is normalized for legibility.

Sheet 1: profile and portrait

John King was a 13 years boy that turn into a pirate when he was 13. John King was an 18-centrey pirate. He joined the crew of Samuel Bellamy while still a juvenile and may have been the youngest knowl pirate record

Beside the profile, a pencil drawing of King: a boy in a head bandana with a spyglass raised to one eye, a buttoned vest, a belt, and boots planted wide.

Sheet 2: notes

  • John was originally a passenger on the ship Bonetta with his mother when Bellamys pirates capture it in 1716 ("when" is inserted above the line with a caret in the original)
  • He sailed abord the famous pirate ship Whyday Gally Bellamys flagship
Three projects and counting — John King is this class's favorite person in the story. The facts here hold up: King boarded the Bonetta as a passenger with his mother in November 1716, demanded to join Bellamy's crew, and at somewhere between eight and eleven years old he may well be the youngest pirate on record — the full account is on his card at People. For the rest of the class's King shelf, see Erick's first-person writing piece and poster.
