The Story of Albus Sharktooth

By Kelly and Jasmely. An invented pirate — no Albus Sharktooth sailed on the Whydah — but a whole imagined life told first-person across two handwritten pages: a Pocumtuck boy forced aboard a pirate ship at thirteen, shipwrecked by a storm at 23, and home with his people until age 92.

The story

Transcribed with the students' wording, spelling, and punctuation preserved; letter case is normalized for legibility. One word we could not read with confidence is marked [?].

Hi my name is Albus Sharktooth, I'm from the Pocumtuck Tribe. I was born in Connecticett River Valley. I used to go to a boarding school but I escaped from that horrible place, I was thirteen that time while I was running away but ended up running into this man name William Bubbles he invited me on his boat; I thought he was nice until he locked me up and threatend to kill me if I didn't become part of his. I kept refusing until he pulled his blade out and held it up to my neck he kept repeating if you don't obey me I will slice your head off your neck, I got so frightend so I just gave in. He made me bottle ships and steal. I tried escaping but he caught me he made his helpers punish me, I ended up killing 267 people and stealing 100 ships I felt so drained and just wanted to leave. One day while sailing the boat we ran into a battel. The other pirates were throwing cannons and shoot us while we're fighting back our boat was sinking I helped fix our boat and hopmason [?]. I've been working for William Bubbles for ten years, I was 23 and still on his ship but then a big storm came in and kept rocking our boat until the top finally bursted open and started sinking rapidly. I finally jump off and got away on the mini boat, I made it to shore and saw a tribe name Pocumtuck. I asked them to take me in and they agreed they gave me new clothes and helped me learn their lifestyle. I finally espected and became apart of the Pocumtuck tribe and stayed with for years. I just turned 92 and I'm spreading my story around before I sadly pass away. Which gets passed down to my family. It was August 5 1901 the day I sadly passed away.

Kelly and Jasmely

The character is invented; the bones of his story are not. The Pocumtuck are a real people of the middle Connecticut River Valley, and pirates really did force captives to serve — the question of who joined willingly and who was forced is exactly what the 1717 Boston trials turned on. A young Indigenous crew member surviving a storm that sinks his ship is the Whydah's own history: John Julian, the ship's sixteen-year-old Miskito pilot, was one of only two men to reach shore alive on April 26, 1717. Where the students' story gives their pirate what history denied Julian — escape, welcome, an old age, and a family to carry the story — that is the point of a perspective piece. The storm itself is at The Wreck, and the forced-versus-willing question at Why Piracy.
