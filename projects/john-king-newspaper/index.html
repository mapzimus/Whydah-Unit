Breaking News: John King

A newspaper front page reporting the death of John King in the Whydah wreck — BREAKING NEWS in hand-drawn bubble letters, a pasted print of Gregory Manchess's painting of the boy pirate, and the cause of death in a boxed bulletin. No name is written on the work.

The front page, piece by piece

Handwritten text is transcribed with the student's wording, spelling, and punctuation preserved; letter case is normalized for legibility.

Headline (bubble letters)

BREAKING NEWS

Thought bubble

John King was a 8-11 year's old he died April 26 1717!

Boxed bulletin

John King Cause of Death: Whydah Gally sank in a violent storm of the coast of CapeCod.

Pasted print

A black-and-white print of Gregory Manchess's painting of John King — the boy standing hands-on-hips in a striped shirt, neckerchief, and tricorn hat — is pasted along the left side, with the artist's credit line visible at the bottom of the print.

The bulletin is accurate reporting. King died when the Whydah Gally sank in a violent storm off Cape Cod on April 26, 1717, and the 8-to-11 age range is the real scientific estimate from his remains — an 11-inch fibula identified in 2006, still in its shoe and silk stocking. Full story on his card at People and the storm at The Wreck. It joins Erick's writing piece and poster, Kelly and Jasmely's fact sheet, and a second front page, the Savage report, on the class's John King shelf — the same Manchess painting also appears mounted on Erick's writing piece.
Presented at the Navigating Piracy Showcase, August 6, 2026, at Collins Middle School, Salem.
