Showcase 2026 · Newspaper front page

Breaking News: Philip Ashton returns

By Destiny. A newspaper front page on Philip Ashton, the Marblehead fisherman kidnapped by the pirate Ned Low — the first project in the archive to take the class's Salem-figure option instead of a Whydah subject. Datelined 5/11/25: in-world, days after Ashton really walked home.

The front page

Handwritten text is transcribed with the student's wording, spelling, and punctuation preserved; letter case is normalized for legibility.

Masthead and headline

BREAKING NEWS!

Man returns after two years and ten months in piracy!

Dateline

Published by Destiny · 5/11/25

Column 1

When Philip Ashton was fishing off the coast of Nova Scotia, Canada and a pirate by the name Edward Low, or Ned Low, captured Philip Ashton and was forced into piracy. Philip Ashton managed to escape and hid in the Bay islands of Honduras Jungle, he had to live 16 months in the Jungle.

Column 2

One day a man by the name Philip Ashton got forced into piracy in June 1722, but he managed to escape in March 1723 when Philip hid in the Bay islands of Honduras Jungle until the pirates decited to depart without him.

Layout

The masthead is drawn in heavy blue bubble letters, the second column opens in bold marker before shifting to pencil, and the unwritten column space is filled with vertical blue rules and wavy lines — a mock newspaper layout, columns and all.

Every number on this front page is right. Ashton was captured off Nova Scotia in June 1722, escaped at Roatán in the Bay Islands of Honduras in March 1723 by hiding in the woods until Low's men sailed without him, survived about sixteen months on the island, and walked through his parents' front door in Marblehead in 1725 — two years and ten months after he was taken, just as the headline says. Even the dateline plays along: 5/11/25 is May 1725, the month of the real homecoming. It's the North Shore's other pirate story — Marblehead is Salem's immediate neighbor — and the full account is on the Salem page, with how the class handled captivity-versus-choice at Why Piracy.
Presented at the Navigating Piracy Showcase, August 6, 2026, at Collins Middle School, Salem.
